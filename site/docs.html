Accept recurring USDC on Arc

Everything a merchant needs to bill subscriptions with Levy: plans, checkout links, webhooks and how to verify them, invoices, and what happens when a payment fails.

Status. Levy runs on Arc testnet. Payments use test USDC and no real money moves. The contracts are not audited and not on mainnet yet; addresses below are the testnet ones. This page describes the next SubscriptionManager (v2), which adds merchant accounts, retiring plans, cycle limits, intro prices and subscription transfers. It is not deployed yet, so the testnet contracts below still work as before v2 (§2).

1. Quickstart

From nothing to a working checkout link on Arc testnet, all in the browser:

  1. A wallet. Use a browser wallet such as MetaMask. The app asks it to add Arc Testnet the first time it sends a transaction.
  2. Test USDC. Get some at faucet.circle.com (choose Arc Testnet). On Arc, USDC also pays the network fee, so you need no other token.
  3. Sign in. Open app.getlevy.xyz and sign in with that wallet. Signing is free and sends no transaction. This wallet owns your merchant account, and your payments go to it unless you set a payout address (§3).
  4. Create a plan. Under Plans, choose New plan: a price, a billing cycle, an optional free trial and how many failed payments to allow before the subscription is past due. Optionally, a number of payments, an intro price for the first payments, and whether customers may move the subscription to another wallet. Creating it is one transaction from your wallet.
  5. Share the checkout link. Copy the link next to your plan and send it to customers. Add &ref= and your own customer id to tie each subscription to a customer (§4).
  6. Get notified. Under Webhooks, add your endpoint and choose events, then check every delivery's signature as in §6.
  7. Watch it run. Subscriptions, invoices and the dunning queue appear in the dashboard. Your customers manage their subscriptions at app.getlevy.xyz/portal.html.

3. Plans

A plan is a price, a billing cycle and a few terms, stored on-chain under your merchant account. The simplest way to create one is New plan in the dashboard, which checks the limits below and shows what customers will pay. Underneath, it calls:

createPlan(
  account,         // address, the merchant account the plan belongs to: your own address to start
  (
    price,         // uint256, per-cycle price in 6-decimal USDC units
    cycleSeconds,  // uint256, billing cycle length in seconds
    trialSeconds,  // uint256, trial length in seconds (0 for none)
    maxFailed,     // uint8, failed payments before the subscription goes PAST_DUE
    maxCycles,     // uint32, payments before the subscription completes (0 for no limit)
    introPrice,    // uint256, price of each intro payment (0 when introCycles is 0)
    introCycles,   // uint32, how many payments cost introPrice (0 for none)
    transferable   // bool, whether customers may move a subscription to another wallet
  )
)

The contract requires that you own account, a price above 0, a cycle from 1 day (86400) to 10 years, a trial shorter than the cycle, and maxFailed from 1 to 255. An intro price must be above 0 and below the price, and on a limited plan introCycles may not exceed maxCycles. Common cycles: weekly 604800, monthly 2592000 (30 days), quarterly 7776000 (90 days), yearly 31536000 (365 days). The call emits PlanCreated with your planId; GET /api/plans lists every plan.

  • price is what the customer pays each cycle once any intro payments are used up. It is fixed: to change a price, create a new plan, send new customers to it and retire the old one.
  • cycleSeconds is how often a payment is collected. "Monthly" is a fixed 30 days, so billing dates drift against calendar months.
  • trialSeconds delays the first charge. With 0, the first cycle is charged the moment the customer subscribes.
  • maxFailed is how many failed payments a subscription tolerates before it is past due. At twice this number it pauses itself. 2 or 3 fits most businesses.
  • maxCycles is how many payments a subscription makes. The last one completes it: the status becomes COMPLETED, which is final, and the subscription is paid through the end of that cycle. 0 means no limit, 12 sells 12 installments or a fixed term, and 1 with no trial is a one-time payment, collected and completed when the customer subscribes. Customers can still cancel early.
  • introPrice and introCycles price a subscription's first payments lower. A trial comes first and does not count, and after a plan change the new plan bills its full price. The intro price is per subscription, so a customer who cancels and subscribes again gets it again: check eligibility by ref and your own records, as for trials.
  • transferable lets customers move a subscription on this plan to another wallet (§11).

Retiring a plan

setPlanRetired(planId, true), or Retire in the dashboard, closes a plan to new customers: subscribing and plan changes into it revert RetiredPlan, and its checkout link says the plan is closed. Its subscribers keep paying as before and can still change out of it. Reopen it with setPlanRetired(planId, false). A launch promotion is an intro plan you retire when the promotion ends.

Your merchant account

The address that creates a plan for itself is a merchant account, and that address stays its permanent id in webhooks and the API. Its owner acts for it: creates plans, pauses, resumes, cancels and retires, and signs in to its dashboard. The owner starts as the account address itself. Its payout is where the 99% goes, which follows the owner unless the owner fixes it elsewhere, such as at a Safe, with setPayout(account, payout); setPayout(account, 0x0) follows the owner again, from the next payment either way. To hand the account to another wallet, the owner calls transferOwnership(account, newOwner) and the new owner calls acceptOwnership(account), so a mistyped address never takes control. A fixed payout stays; one that follows the owner moves with it. The previous owner then loses the account's dashboard and webhooks. The dashboard's Merchant account panel does all of this, and every change sends merchant.updated. If USDC blocklists your payout address, your charges revert until you set a new one. Then one charge collects one cycle, and any further cycles missed meanwhile are never collected.

4. Checkout links and references

Send customers your plan's checkout link:

https://app.getlevy.xyz/checkout.html?plan=<planId>&ref=<reference>

It shows the plan with its intro price and number of payments, connects the customer's wallet, explains the allowance and takes two wallet confirmations: an approval for the number of cycles they choose (12 by default, added to any Levy allowance they already have), then the subscription. The approval prices intro payments at the intro price, and for a limited plan it covers at most the plan's payments, all of them by default. Without a trial, the first cycle is charged right away. The page ends on a receipt. A retired plan's link says the plan is closed.

ref is how you match a subscription to your own customer. It is stored with the subscription on-chain and sent on every webhook (null when none was given). It can be 0x plus 64 hex digits, or up to 31 printable ASCII characters, which are left-aligned and zero-padded. The webhook payload always carries it as 0x plus 64 lowercase hex digits, so cust42 arrives as 0x637573743432 followed by zeros; decode it up to the first zero byte. Two rules:

  • It is public. Anyone can read it on-chain, so never use an email, a name or anything personal. Use an opaque id.
  • Whoever subscribes chooses it. Someone could subscribe with another customer's reference and then cancel, making that customer look cancelled. Issue an unguessable reference per checkout (for example 32 random bytes you store against the customer), and once subscription.created links a reference to a customer, track that subscription by its subId.

Building your own flow

Direct approval, two transactions. The customer calls approve on USDC for the SubscriptionManager, for at least the first payment (the intro price, if the plan has one), then subscribe(planId, ref) on the SubscriptionManager. Without a trial this charges the first cycle at once, and fails if it cannot.

Permit2, one transaction after a one-time setup. The customer has approved Permit2 for USDC once (shared with every app that uses Permit2), signs an EIP-712 Permit2 message that lets the SubscriptionManager pull USDC, and submits subscribeWithPermit2(planId, ref, permitSingle, signature) from the same wallet. Only the signer can submit it.

5. Webhooks

Levy signs every billing event with Ed25519 and POSTs it to your endpoint. Add endpoints under Webhooks in the dashboard, or from your backend (§9):

POST /api/webhooks
Authorization: Bearer <token>
Content-Type: application/json

{
  "url": "https://your-domain.com/hooks/levy",
  "events": ["charge.succeeded", "charge.failed", "dunning.started", "subscription.cancelled"]
}

The endpoint must be https on port 443 at a public address. Levy refuses private, loopback and cloud-metadata addresses and does not follow redirects, so register the final URL. Up to 5 endpoints per merchant account; add "merchant" to the body to register for an account you own other than your own address. Answer within 5 seconds and do slow work after acknowledging.

Every payload except merchant.updated carries subId, merchant (your merchant account) and ref, plus:

EventExtra payload fields
subscription.createdsubscriber, planId, nextChargeAt
subscription.updatedthe customer changed plan: oldPlanId, newPlanId, credit, txHash, blockNumber
charge.succeededamount, fee (6-decimal units; amount is 0 when credit paid the cycle), invoiceRef, chargeCount, txHash, blockNumber
invoice.readythe same body as charge.succeeded, sent alongside it
charge.failedfailedAttempts, txHash, blockNumber
dunning.startedfirst failure only: nextRetryAt (unix seconds)
subscription.pausedauto: true when the contract paused it after repeated failures, false when you or the customer paused it
subscription.resumednone
subscription.cancellednone
subscription.completedthe last payment of a limited plan was collected: txHash, blockNumber. The payment is the charge.succeeded from the same transaction
subscription.transferredthe subscription moved to another wallet, which pays from now on: from, to, txHash, blockNumber
allowance.lowsubscriber, allowance, balance, cyclesCovered, nextChargeAt
merchant.updatedyour account's owner, pending owner or payout changed. No subId or ref: merchant, owner, payout (null while payments follow the owner), pendingOwner (null when none), txHash, blockNumber

subscription.resumed and subscription.cancelled have the same body: use the signed X-Levy-Event header to tell them apart. allowance.low is your cue to email the customer before payments start failing. The allowance is one pool shared by all of a customer's Levy subscriptions; the warning is sent once per subscription, and again only after a top-up runs low again. Intro payments count at the intro price, and a limited plan whose remaining payments are covered is never low. Allowances granted through Permit2 are not counted yet, so a Permit2 subscriber is flagged low from the start.

Failed deliveries are retried after 1 minute, 5 minutes, 15 minutes and 1 hour, then every 6 hours. A delivery whose next retry would pass 24 hours of age is dropped instead, so the last attempt comes about 19 hours 21 minutes after the first. The dashboard shows each endpoint's delivery log. Disabling an endpoint drops what is queued for it, and nothing is queued while it is off.

6. Verify webhook signatures

Every delivery carries these headers:

  • X-Levy-Signature: base64 Ed25519 signature over {timestamp}.{deliveryId}.{webhookId}.{event}.{raw body}
  • X-Levy-Timestamp: unix seconds when this attempt was signed (retries get a new one)
  • X-Levy-Delivery: the delivery id, 16 hex digits, a dash and a number; the same across retries and never reused, so store it and ignore repeats
  • X-Levy-Webhook: the id of the endpoint the delivery was made for; yours is returned at registration and shown in the dashboard
  • X-Levy-Event: the event type, for example charge.succeeded
  • X-Levy-Key-Id: which signing key was used

One key signs for every merchant, so a valid signature proves Levy sent a delivery, not that it was sent to you. Checking it against your own webhook id and merchant account stops someone forwarding you a delivery made for their endpoint. The public key, its keyId and the event list are at GET https://app.getlevy.xyz/api/webhooks/public-key. For every delivery:

  1. Read the raw body exactly as received, before parsing it.
  2. Reject it if X-Levy-Timestamp is more than 5 minutes from your clock, so a captured delivery cannot be replayed.
  3. Reject it if X-Levy-Event is not one of the thirteen event types, or X-Levy-Delivery is not 16 hex digits, a dash and a number. Event names contain dots, and these checks stop the signed text being split at a different dot.
  4. Verify X-Levy-Signature over {X-Levy-Timestamp}.{X-Levy-Delivery}.{your webhook id}.{X-Levy-Event}.{raw body} with the public key, using the id you stored for this endpoint, not the header.
  5. Ignore it if you already processed that X-Levy-Delivery id. The id is signed, so this is safe.
  6. After parsing, reject the event if merchant is not your merchant account, and act only on subscriptions to your own plans.

In Node.js, with no dependencies:

import { createPublicKey, verify } from "node:crypto";

const API = "https://app.getlevy.xyz";
const WEBHOOK_ID = "1"; // this endpoint's id, from registration or the dashboard
const { publicKey } = await fetch(API + "/api/webhooks/public-key").then((r) => r.json());
const key = createPublicKey(publicKey);
const EVENTS = new Set(["subscription.created", "subscription.updated", "charge.succeeded",
  "charge.failed", "dunning.started", "subscription.paused", "subscription.resumed",
  "subscription.cancelled", "invoice.ready", "allowance.low", "subscription.completed",
  "subscription.transferred", "merchant.updated"]);

// rawBody: the request body as a string, before JSON parsing.
function isValid(rawBody, headers) {
  const ts = headers["x-levy-timestamp"];
  const event = headers["x-levy-event"];
  const delivery = headers["x-levy-delivery"];
  if (Math.abs(Date.now() / 1000 - Number(ts)) > 300) return false;
  if (!EVENTS.has(event) || !/^[0-9a-f]{16}-[0-9]+$/.test(delivery)) return false;
  const signed = Buffer.from(`${ts}.${delivery}.${WEBHOOK_ID}.${event}.${rawBody}`, "utf-8");
  return verify(null, signed, key, Buffer.from(headers["x-levy-signature"], "base64"));
}

Levy signs with one key per deployment. Trust a new key only when the public-key endpoint returns it.

7. Invoices

Every successful charge is an invoice record: reference, amount, fee, transaction hash, block number and charge count. Export CSV in the dashboard downloads them all; from your backend:

GET /api/invoices
GET /api/invoices?subscription=3
GET /api/invoices?format=csv

The CSV columns are id, invoiceRef, subId, subscriber, merchant, amountUsdc, feeUsdc, txHash, blockNumber, chargeCount, with amounts in decimal USDC.

8. When a payment fails

A payment fails when the customer's USDC balance or allowance is too low. The contract never reverts on a failed payment: it records the failure on-chain and emits ChargeFailed, never sooner than the schedule below: the contract enforces its gaps, so nobody calling charge can pause a subscription faster. Then:

  1. Levy retries 1 day after the first failure, 3 days after the second, and every 7 days after that.
  2. When failures reach the plan's maxFailed, the subscription is past due. It stays chargeable, and Levy keeps retrying.
  3. At twice maxFailed, the contract pauses it. Nothing more is collected until the customer resumes it in the portal; you cannot resume this pause, and your dashboard shows customer resumes instead. Billing then restarts from the resume date, and the missed cycle is not collected.
  4. A successful payment at any point clears the failure count and starts a fresh cycle that day: the billing date moves to the recovery date, and the late days are not billed.

You get charge.failed on every failure, dunning.started on the first and subscription.paused if the contract pauses it. Levy sends no emails to your customers; use these events to send your own. A customer fixes a failing subscription by adding USDC or topping up the allowance in the portal, and the next retry collects.

9. Sign in from your backend

The dashboard signs you in with your wallet. A backend does the same with Sign-In with Ethereum, using the wallet that owns your merchant account (an ordinary wallet; contract wallets are not supported yet):

POST /api/auth/challenge   {"address": "0xYourWallet"}
  -> {"nonce": "...", "message": "..."}

sign "message" with the wallet (personal_sign)

POST /api/auth/verify      {"nonce": "...", "signature": "0x..."}
  -> {"token": "...", "address": "0x...", "expiresAt": 1790000000}

then send on every call:   Authorization: Bearer <token>

A challenge expires after 5 minutes and works once. A token lasts 24 hours; POST /api/auth/logout revokes it. Every endpoint returns only your own data. The merchant endpoints act for your own address's account unless you pass merchant (in the query, or in the body of POST /api/webhooks) naming another account, and the signed-in wallet must own that account now, or the answer is 403.

11. FAQ

How do I change a price? Create a new plan, send new customers to it and retire the old one. Plan terms cannot be edited, so existing subscriptions keep their price until the customer changes plan in the portal.

How do refunds work? They are your policy and happen off-chain: send USDC back from your own wallet. The contract has no refund function, and a cancelled or completed subscription stays that way.

How do I cancel or pause a customer? Use Pause, Resume or Cancel on the subscription in your dashboard; each is a transaction from the wallet that owns your account. You can resume only a pause you made; a customer's own pause, or one after failed payments, is theirs to resume. Customers can pause, resume and cancel in the portal, and move to another of your plans that is not retired with Change plan. Nothing is charged while a subscription is paused, and cancelling is final.

Can a customer move a subscription to a new wallet? Only on a plan created as transferable. The customer offers it with proposeTransfer(subId, newWallet) and the new wallet accepts with acceptTransfer(subId, planId), both from the portal. It keeps its id, plan, reference, paid time and credit, and later payments come from the new wallet. Only the customer can offer it and only that wallet can accept, so nobody can make a wallet pay without its consent. You receive subscription.transferred; keep tracking it by subId.

What does the 1% fee apply to? Each collected payment: the contract sends 99% to your payout address and 1% to the protocol in the same transaction. Failed payments cost nothing, and nothing is owed when nothing is collected.

Who pays gas for charges? Levy's keeper pays for the charges it submits. Anyone may call charge once a payment is due, but you never need to.

Can Levy take more than the plan price? No. Each billing date, the contract can collect the plan's price (or its intro price) once, only for that plan's merchant account, and never before the date, and a limited plan stops after its last payment. It can never take more than the allowance the customer approved.
