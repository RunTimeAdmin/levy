# Levy repository review

**Reviewed:** 2 October 2026 · Private [RunTimeAdmin/levy](https://github.com/RunTimeAdmin/levy) at [`a7fb2bf`](https://github.com/RunTimeAdmin/levy/commit/a7fb2bfa73d2aa5159d40030231605c4d91929e1). This is a code, test, documentation, public-site, and read-only testnet review—not a paid audit or a production penetration test. I made **no source edits, deployments, or transactions**.

## Bottom line

Levy is a real, coherent **Arc-native USDC subscription billing system**, not just a landing page: immutable-price on-chain plans, subscriber-approved allowances, a permissionless charge entry point, a keeper/indexer, dunning, a merchant API/dashboard, customer portal, checkout, signed webhooks and fee routing. Its fee is 1% of amounts collected; successful pulls pay the merchant and fee router atomically. The code and tests are stronger than the maturity of the public rollout. **Keep it in testnet; do not represent it as mainnet-ready yet.**

Most important: the public site says the week-long live billing soak is underway, but on **2 October** the advertised deployed testnet manager had `planCount() = 0` and `subCount() = 0`, while the public `/api/plans` returned `[]`. The deployed addresses contain bytecode and the API is healthy, but no plan or subscriber has gone through a billing cycle on that deployment. This is a verified **release-readiness gap**, not evidence of a broken keeper or failed payment. Update the public wording until a real soak has been run and recorded.

Separately, **self-service checkout needs transaction recovery before mainnet**. If a successful subscription transaction's receipt times out, the page may invite a retry that creates a second paid subscription. An ambiguous *approval* can also cause the page to add the chosen budget to an allowance that was already increased. These are source-traced failure paths, not observed live incidents.

## Findings and product gaps

### 1. Live billing soak has not started on the advertised manager — release blocker (verified)

The deployed manager at `0x33c8e7e46583bdfd2e001f55ac1c58bd84eb739f` has **zero plans and zero subscriptions** according to direct read-only `planCount()` and `subCount()` calls over `https://rpc.testnet.arc.io`. The live [plans endpoint](https://app.getlevy.xyz/api/plans) also returns `[]`. The [soak plan](https://github.com/RunTimeAdmin/levy/blob/a7fb2bfa73d2aa5159d40030231605c4d91929e1/ops/testnet-soak.md#pass-criteria) has its customer, seven-day, webhook, reconciliation, and fee-sweep checks uncompleted; its log has setup entries, not live subscription results. The [site](https://getlevy.xyz/) currently says the week-long soak is running. **Action:** create a real merchant plan and subscriber wallets, execute the documented paths and daily measurements for seven days, reconcile on-chain charges against API/invoices and webhook receipts, publish a testnet results summary, and fix the site's status copy now. Avoid using faucet-funded demo activity as proof of production demand.

### 2. Public callers can accelerate failed-payment suspension — medium (code and existing test verified)

[`charge()`](https://github.com/RunTimeAdmin/levy/blob/a7fb2bfa73d2aa5159d40030231605c4d91929e1/src/SubscriptionManager.sol#L221-L249) is permissionless. Failed pulls are spaced by only one day in [`_recordFailure`](https://github.com/RunTimeAdmin/levy/blob/a7fb2bfa73d2aa5159d40030231605c4d91929e1/src/SubscriptionManager.sol#L291-L305). The keeper's intended 1/3/7-day dunning intervals live off-chain. With `maxFailed = 2` and an unfunded due subscription, anyone can record failures on four consecutive days, triggering automatic pause three days after the first failure, earlier than the keeper's longer retry schedule. The repository's [regression test](https://github.com/RunTimeAdmin/levy/blob/a7fb2bfa73d2aa5159d40030231605c4d91929e1/test/FailureCooldown.t.sol#L81-L102) demonstrates exactly this sequence. **Impact:** not theft and not an attack on a funded customer, but premature service interruption and dunning notifications for a customer who could have topped up during the promised grace period. **Action:** either enforce the intended failure schedule on-chain without delaying successful retries, or explicitly adopt daily failure cadence everywhere and tell merchants the shorter suspension timetable. Test both griefing and a successful top-up inside the cooldown.

### 3. Unlimited self-funded low-value subscriptions can consume keeper gas — medium operational risk (documented, still open)

Anyone can create one-day, one-microunit plans and subscribe to themselves; the fee rounds to zero and an active, funded subscription never auto-pauses. Across enough accounts/subscriptions this can impose unlimited daily charge transactions on Levy's keeper. The one-day minimum cycle prevents a per-second version, but does not cap total work. See [`MIN_CYCLE` and plan creation](https://github.com/RunTimeAdmin/levy/blob/a7fb2bfa73d2aa5159d40030231605c4d91929e1/src/SubscriptionManager.sol#L24-L35) and [the project's own security review](https://github.com/RunTimeAdmin/levy/blob/a7fb2bfa73d2aa5159d40030231605c4d91929e1/SECURITY_REVIEW.md#L40-L69). **Action before scaling:** implement an explicit keeper service policy for unaffiliated/uneconomic plans—minimum net fee, merchant allowlisting during launch, paid admission, quotas, or merchant-funded keeper gas—and load-test backlog and costs. The contract may remain permissionless while the *operator-paid keeper* chooses what it services; disclose that distinction.

### 4. Deployment validates dependency shape, not canonical identity — medium operational risk

[`Deploy.s.sol`](https://github.com/RunTimeAdmin/levy/blob/a7fb2bfa73d2aa5159d40030231605c4d91929e1/script/Deploy.s.sol#L29-L72) defaults to Arc USDC and Permit2, checks that each address has code and that USDC has six decimals, and requires a contract treasury on Arc mainnet. It still accepts environment overrides that point at any six-decimal token and any code-bearing Permit2 address; both become immutable in the manager. A privileged deployment mistake or compromised environment would require migration and subscriber reapproval. **Action:** pin/allowlist canonical addresses on chain ID 5042, explicitly review overrides for testnets only, and verify constructor immutables, router destination, decimals, chain ID and source bytecode after deployment. This is a configuration hazard, not an untrusted-caller exploit.

### 5. Checkout lacks verified merchant identity and service terms — high-priority trust/conversion gap

The checkout identifies the merchant by an address and plan number, plus price, cadence, first-charge date, network and manager address ([`checkout.js:38–68`](https://github.com/RunTimeAdmin/levy/blob/a7fb2bfa73d2aa5159d40030231605c4d91929e1/api/public/checkout.js#L38-L68)). There is no customer-visible, authenticated merchant name, product description, verified website or explicit cancellation/refund terms. Anyone can create a plan for their own payout address and send a checkout link; an address alone is not understandable to most buyers. **Action:** add signed merchant metadata tied to the on-chain payout address and plan ID, prove domain control, display a clearly verified merchant card and plain-language recurring-payment terms before approval, and include those terms in a downloadable authorization receipt. Do not treat an unverified name fetched from a merchant-controlled API as proof of identity.

### 6. Permit2 is not yet a first-use one-click checkout — documented/UX gap

The [merchant guide](https://github.com/RunTimeAdmin/levy/blob/a7fb2bfa73d2aa5159d40030231605c4d91929e1/docs/merchant-guide.md#L192-L199) correctly says Permit2's one-transaction subscription follows a **one-time USDC approval to Permit2**. The browser checkout implements the direct approval-and-subscribe route, not a Permit2 path. On a fresh wallet, a Permit2 signature alone cannot pull USDC; with a trial the failure may not surface until the first renewal. The underlying allowance warning also reads only the direct manager allowance, so Permit2 customers can appear low when they are not. **Action:** test against canonical Arc Permit2 and real testnet USDC, show approval prerequisites and allowance runway accurately, and only then consider a Permit2/permit-based accelerated flow. Do not market it as first-use one-click today.

### 7. Checkout rounds USDC display down to cents — low correctness issue (reproduced)

[`checkout.js:4–7`](https://github.com/RunTimeAdmin/levy/blob/a7fb2bfa73d2aa5159d40030231605c4d91929e1/api/public/checkout.js#L4-L7) slices the 6-decimal amount to two decimals. On-chain plans have no minimum price beyond one unit; a plan costing 9,999 microunits (`$0.009999`) is shown as `$0.00`, as are its budget and receipt. This is user-facing disclosure inaccuracy even though the dollar difference is small. **Action:** show all meaningful digits below one cent, otherwise use an explicitly rounded **approx.** value and preserve the exact six-decimal amount in the approval/receipt summary. Add tests for micro-prices and 29.000001 USDC.

### 8. Duplicate paid subscriptions after an uncertain checkout retry — medium customer-billing risk (source-traced)

The contract accepts a new [`_subscribe()`](https://github.com/RunTimeAdmin/levy/blob/a7fb2bfa73d2aa5159d40030231605c4d91929e1/src/SubscriptionManager.sol#L192-L219) on every valid call and bills the first cycle immediately on no-trial plans. After broadcasting a transaction, [`wallet.js:82–94`](https://github.com/RunTimeAdmin/levy/blob/a7fb2bfa73d2aa5159d40030231605c4d91929e1/api/public/wallet.js#L82-L94) keeps its hash only in a local variable and waits up to 120 seconds for its receipt; even one receipt-RPC error throws and discards the hash from the caller's perspective. [`checkout.js:143–147`](https://github.com/RunTimeAdmin/levy/blob/a7fb2bfa73d2aa5159d40030231605c4d91929e1/api/public/checkout.js#L143-L147) re-enables Subscribe. If the first transaction was included but its receipt was unavailable, a second click or tab can create a second paid, recurring subscription. On a trial, this creates two future obligations instead. Worse, if the *approval* succeeded but its receipt was unavailable, checkout still considers it unapproved and adds `price × cycles` **on top of the already-increased allowance** during retry ([`checkout.js:124–130`](https://github.com/RunTimeAdmin/levy/blob/a7fb2bfa73d2aa5159d40030231605c4d91929e1/api/public/checkout.js#L124-L130)). This is not an observed live incident; multiple subscriptions may be intentional for some buyers, so do not globally forbid them. **Action:** persist the submitted hash immediately, resume polling after reload, and treat a receipt error as an *unknown outcome*, never permission to rebroadcast. Show a recovery/pending state with explorer link, reconcile existing wallet/plan/order before retry, and use an explicit unique checkout/order ID if contract-level idempotency is added. Test approval timeout, subscribe timeout, reload and two tabs.

### 9. Wallet connection does not recheck chain after switch/add — low (reproduced with a provider simulation)

[`LevyWallet.connect()`](https://github.com/RunTimeAdmin/levy/blob/a7fb2bfa73d2aa5159d40030231605c4d91929e1/api/public/wallet.js#L60-L76) reads the chain before `wallet_switchEthereumChain` or `wallet_addEthereumChain`, but never confirms that the resulting chain actually equals the requested Arc chain. A provider can report a successful add without switching, after which checkout calls and transactions use Arc-configured contract addresses on another chain. This was reproduced in an isolated provider simulation; impact with ordinary compliant wallets is lower, and a malicious provider is itself a trust boundary. **Action:** read `eth_chainId` again after either switch/add and fail closed if it differs. Cover a successful switch, an unsuccessful switch, and an add-without-switch in browser-wallet tests.

### 10. Restoring a lost keeper database can strand past-due customers — known recovery risk

The keeper [backfills subscription state](https://github.com/RunTimeAdmin/levy/blob/a7fb2bfa73d2aa5159d40030231605c4d91929e1/keeper/src/index.ts#L21-L26), but a deleted SQLite file loses dunning rows. The [ordinary due query excludes `PAST_DUE`](https://github.com/RunTimeAdmin/levy/blob/a7fb2bfa73d2aa5159d40030231605c4d91929e1/keeper/src/db.ts#L260-L274); the [retry query](https://github.com/RunTimeAdmin/levy/blob/a7fb2bfa73d2aa5159d40030231605c4d91929e1/keeper/src/db.ts#L298-L321) only sees surviving dunning rows. A past-due customer may therefore stop receiving retries after database loss even though the contract remains chargeable. This is correctly [documented by Levy](https://github.com/RunTimeAdmin/levy/blob/a7fb2bfa73d2aa5159d40030231605c4d91929e1/keeper/README.md#L82-L84), not a newly discovered exploit. **Action:** treat SQLite as production-critical, back it up and test restoration; reconstruct missing dunning state conservatively from on-chain `failedAttempts`/`lastFailedAt` after a full deployment-block rescan. Add a DB-loss integration test and a stuck-`PAST_DUE` alert.

## What is working well

The contracts have a small attack surface: fixed merchant payout per immutable-price plan, per-cycle timing, atomic routing, no operator-only charge power, subscriber-controlled cancel and allowance revocation, scoped same-merchant proration and bounded failure recording. The repository already documents meaningful edge cases instead of hiding them. The API uses signed, origin-bound wallet authentication with secure page cookies, scoped queries, content-security policies and CSRF controls. Webhooks sign timestamp, delivery ID, event and raw body; endpoint checks resist redirects and private-address destinations. Charging is isolated from slow merchant webhooks.

## Release sequence and product differentiation

1. **Truth in status and first real billing cycle:** update the landing page, then create a real testnet plan and record all seven days of charges, trials, failures, plan changes, webhooks and reconciled invoices with dates and tx hashes. Require a signed-off results sheet rather than counting elapsed calendar days from deployment.

1. **Close the reliability and customer-protection gaps:** make checkout broadcast/recovery idempotent from the buyer's perspective, including approval ambiguity; choose and enforce an honest dunning policy; set a keeper-spam admission policy; fix wallet chain verification and exact price display; add signed merchant identity and clear cancellation/refund terms.

1. **Mainnet gate:** independently verify Arc USDC and Permit2 addresses, Safe treasury signer/process and deployment config; commission an external contract + off-chain audit; complete real-Arc Permit2, webhook replay, database-loss/recovery, restart and USDC/blocklist exercises. Back up SQLite, reconstruct missing past-due retries, and document an immutable-contract migration/reauthorization plan.

1. **Differentiation:** don't call recurring stablecoin billing itself unique. [Stripe already announced wallet-authorized USDC subscriptions](https://stripe.com/blog/introducing-stablecoin-payments-for-subscriptions) in 2025 (Base and Polygon, US private preview). Levy can instead own the **Arc-native transparent subscription mandate**: a trustworthy merchant identity, exact authorization/cap, upcoming billing calendar, shared allowance runway, alert-before-failure, one-click pause/revoke and verifiable on-chain receipt in a single customer portal. An SDK and webhook-replay sandbox would make merchant integration significantly easier. Evaluate these with real merchants before introducing a token or more billing mechanics.

**Scope limitations:** Tests here are local and controlled; I did not sign in as a merchant, execute live transactions, inspect the private server, measure ongoing keeper uptime, verify smart-contract source on an explorer, or conduct a formal audit. The zero-plan testnet observation is a point-in-time read, not a prediction of future state.
